Existing assets · The number most often wrong

Your 2029 Safeguard position may be calculated the wrong way

Most projections of a facility’s 2029 compliance gap apply the annual decline rate to the baseline and stop there. The Safeguard Rules do not work that way, and the difference is not a rounding error.

The floor

Baselines stop declining at 100,000 t

The Rules set a statutory floor. In the most recent published register, 43 facilities sit exactly on it. A projection that declines straight through the floor overstates the gap — on one facility we tested, by almost seven times.

The dead zone

Abatement that earns no credit

Credits are issued against the baseline as it would have been without the floor. Between the two lines there is a band where a facility can abate and receive nothing for it. If you are on the floor, you need to know how wide that band is before you cost a single project.

The consequence

It is a material number

Priced against a revenue proxy, 2029 compliance cost runs to several per cent of revenue at the more exposed operations we have costed — enough to change what an asset is worth, not just what it reports.

The Pre-DD workflow

The F1-F4 Pre-DD Framework

Each project is screened through four connected questions using public and early project information. Together they test whether a hybrid energy idea is ready to advance — or what has to be resolved first.

F1

Approval Delta

Does the project fit within the existing approval pathway, or does it trigger new issues around disturbance, MMP / licence variation, water, fire, heritage, biodiversity, closure or ownership chain?

F2

Site Suitability

Where could PV, BESS, PCS and interconnection equipment realistically sit, and what constraints are visible from public maps, mine layout, watercourses, roads, pipelines and closure areas?

F3

Microgrid Boundary

How would PV, BESS, diesel generation, PCS, EMS and mine loads connect, and what SLD, genset, switchboard, control, metering and load data is required before reliability can be assessed?

F4

Financeability Readiness

Which commercial structure is worth validating, and what must be proven before the project can move toward EPC pricing, term sheet, lender model, CP mapping or financial close preparation?

About

Heliovulcan is an independent industrial intelligence practice working on industrial energy assets and the projects proposed for them.

The work starts from the same question in both cases:

What can actually be shown from the public record — and what cannot?

Heliovulcan works on facility-level energy and emissions analysis, public-information project screening, hybrid energy systems, PV+BESS feasibility, and financeability assessment for industrial and remote energy users.

The objective is not to replace detailed engineering, environmental studies or bank-grade due diligence. Instead, the goal is to identify critical questions early, narrow the pathway and help owners and investors understand where the major risks and opportunities are likely to sit.

The approach combines technical modelling, project-finance thinking and structured due-diligence frameworks to provide an independent early view of complex industrial energy questions.

Point of view

A model is not a project.

A financeable project is a resolved bundle of site, approval, connection, contract and cashflow. The purpose of an early screen is not a beautiful IRR — it is finding where the project breaks while the answer is still cheap to act on.
