Asset & market intelligence

What is actually true about an industrial operation

Before anything is proposed for a plant or a mine there is a prior question, usually assumed rather than answered: what does this operation consume, burn, emit and owe — and who controls it? The same machinery scales up from one facility to a sector.

Energy

What it burns and what it buys

Fuel mix and quantity, self-generation versus purchased electricity, reconstructed average load, and network position. Fuel type is established from evidence, not assumed from the industry code — an assumption that is wrong often enough to matter.

Control

Who can actually decide

Ownership and operator structure, including the common case where the power station belongs to a third party under a long-term agreement and its fuel is reported under a different industry entirely.

Position

Where it sits in its sector

The asset compared against peers on measures that are genuinely comparable, and summed with them to test whether the sector view holds. Both directions, because each catches errors the other does not.

A worked example: the 2029 Safeguard number that is most often wrong

Most projections of a facility’s 2029 compliance gap apply the annual decline rate to the baseline and stop there. The Safeguard Rules do not work that way, and the difference is not a rounding error. This is one question the evidence base answers — shown here because it demonstrates the method rather than because it is the only thing on offer.

The floor

Baselines stop declining at 100,000 t

The Rules set a statutory floor. In the most recent published register, 43 facilities sit exactly on it. A projection that declines straight through the floor overstates the gap — on one facility we tested, by almost seven times.

The dead zone

Abatement that earns no credit

Credits are issued against the baseline as it would have been without the floor. Between the two lines there is a band where a facility can abate and receive nothing for it. If you are on the floor, you need to know how wide that band is before you cost a single project.

The consequence

It is a material number

Priced against a revenue proxy, 2029 compliance cost runs to several per cent of revenue at the more exposed operations we have costed — enough to change what an asset is worth, not just what it reports.

Project analysis

When the question is a project that does not exist yet

One application of the same evidence engine, and the one with the longest worked record. It applies when something is being proposed — a mine microgrid, a diesel displacement, a solar-plus-storage concept — and the question is whether it can advance rather than what is already true of the operation hosting it. The output is a decision and a data-room request list, not a forecast.

Four connected questions: the F1–F4 workflow

The method for this route specifically — not the architecture of the platform. Four questions answered from public and early project information, together testing whether an idea is ready to advance, or what has to be resolved first.

F1

Approval Delta

Does the project fit within the existing approval pathway, or does it trigger new issues around disturbance, MMP / licence variation, water, fire, heritage, biodiversity, closure or ownership chain?

F2

Site Suitability

Where could PV, BESS, PCS and interconnection equipment realistically sit, and what constraints are visible from public maps, mine layout, watercourses, roads, pipelines and closure areas?

F3

Microgrid Boundary

How would PV, BESS, diesel generation, PCS, EMS and mine loads connect, and what SLD, genset, switchboard, control, metering and load data is required before reliability can be assessed?

F4

Financeability Readiness

Which commercial structure is worth validating, and what must be proven before the project can move toward EPC pricing, term sheet, lender model, CP mapping or financial close preparation?

Worked example: Fountain Head hybrid energy Pre-DD screen

A public-information desktop Pre-DD screen for a remote mine hybrid-energy case. It tests whether a 60% renewable diesel-PV-BESS hybrid pathway is more useful than a short-life standalone case or a 100% renewable upper-bound case, combining financeability, public map interpretation, candidate energy zones, approval delta questions, microgrid boundary questions and owner data-room requirements.

Fountain Head / Pine Creek, Northern Territory — a public-information Pre-DD screen of solar + BESS + diesel hybrid options for a remote gold mine.
Public key takeaways
  • S4 is the scenario selected for hourly and commercial validation.
  • Public EIS-derived layers provide the evidence context.
  • PV and BESS/PCS areas are preliminary desktop search areas, not equipment footprints.
  • The interpreted map separates constraints, review areas and owner-confirmation areas.
  • Further work requires owner data room, hourly dispatch, EPC pricing and specialist DD.

About

Heliovulcan is an independent industrial intelligence practice working across Australian industrial assets, energy and emissions, policy and regulation, and project and investment questions.

The work starts from the same question every time:

What can actually be shown from the public record — and what cannot?

That covers facility-level energy and emissions analysis, market and sector reconciliation, public-information project screening, hybrid energy systems, PV+BESS feasibility, and financeability assessment for industrial and remote energy users.

Discuss a question

Start with the question, not with a scope

Every route starts the same way: a short, non-confidential exchange to establish whether there is a question worth answering and whether the public record can answer it. Nothing beyond that begins without an agreed scope and fee.

A

Asset owners, operators and their lenders

Establishing energy and fuel position, 2029 Safeguard exposure, who owns the power station and what the contract structure implies — for a facility you run, one you are lending against, or one you are considering acquiring.

B

Investors, analysts and advisers

Screening asset or project exposure across a sector or a portfolio, and identifying which claimed savings and risks are genuinely decision-relevant rather than modelled.

C

Policy, government and industry bodies

Understanding facility, sector or portfolio patterns from reconciled public evidence — including which operations a proposed rule actually reaches.

D

Project sponsors, developers and site owners

Diesel displacement, energy security, candidate sites, and preparing an opportunity for owner discussion, data-room access, specialist DD and commercial structuring.

How work is scoped

Step 1 · Any question

Free discovery exchange

A free, non-confidential introductory review to establish whether the question can be answered from the public record at all.

  • No fee
  • A few high-level inputs — a site name is often enough
  • Verbal initial observations
  • No formal report or model
  • Outcome: stop, redirect or scope the next step
Step 2 · Existing assets

Asset or sector review

A paid, scoped analysis: energy and fuel position, 2029 Safeguard exposure including the baseline floor, ownership and operator structure, and sector position.

  • Scope and fee agreed after the discovery exchange
  • Written profile with every figure evidence-graded
  • Reconciliation against independent accounts
  • Owner-data request list for what the record cannot settle
  • Suits an operator, a lender, an acquirer or an analyst
Step 2 · New projects

Scoped Pre-DD review

A paid, scoped engagement using public and agreed early project information across F1 approval delta, F2 site suitability, F3 microgrid boundary and F4 financeability readiness.

  • Scope and fee agreed after the discovery exchange
  • Written F1-F4 readiness view
  • Evidence-gap and data-room request
  • Due-diligence question register
  • Clear next-step pathway
Step 2 · Selected cases

Success-linked structure

For selected early-stage projects, Heliovulcan may consider a flexible commercial structure where interests are aligned around progress.

  • Reduced upfront fee structures may be considered
  • Milestone-based or success-linked fee options
  • Strategic participation where interests align
  • For credible industrial, mining or remote-energy opportunities
  • Available only with clear fit, data access and decision pathway

Helpful inputs for a project or asset question
  1. Site type — mine, industrial facility, logistics site, remote load, or other
  2. Annual electricity use or diesel consumption — approximate figures are fine

  4. Rough load pattern — day/night split, peak demand, or operating hours

None of this is required to start a conversation. For a research, sector or policy question, the question itself is enough.
