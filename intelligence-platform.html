How the analysis works

Understand an industrial asset or energy project before committing capital

We bring together information that normally sits in separate records: asset ownership, energy use, emissions, approvals, location, infrastructure and project status. We then check the result against independent datasets, and separate confirmed facts from calculations, assumptions and missing information.

How the records connect

How the information connects

Questions about policy, investment, assets and energy often depend on the same underlying information. A facility belongs to a company, uses energy, reports emissions, operates under approvals and may host one or more projects. We link those records so that each analysis starts from a consistent account of the asset.

Company ↔Facility ↔Project ↔Energy ↔Emissions ↔Grid ↔Technology ↔Approval ↔Capital

The value comes from showing how the records relate to one another, not from collecting them in isolation. Each relationship is supported by a source and given an evidence label.

Four ways the information can be used

The same records, used four different ways. What changes is where the question starts, not the underlying data.

Policy Which facilities are exposed? Identify which facilities are covered by a rule and estimate the exposure where it applies. Facilities we could not resolve are reported separately.
Investment Which projects warrant deeper diligence? Screen a portfolio or project pipeline for approval, grid and commercial risks that may require deeper due diligence.
Asset What can we establish about this facility? Build a facility-level view of energy use, emissions, ownership and sector position, then check it against independent accounts.
Energy Where is a technology worth testing? Test where a technology may be technically and commercially viable, given the site, grid and funding constraints.
What this is, and is not

This is an internal research system, not client-operated software and not a public database, and not a substitute for formal due diligence. Coverage grows as assets and sectors are analysed; it is not a live or complete national dataset. The count of what has been analysed in depth is much smaller than the count of what has been mapped as background context.

Featured use case

Understanding an operating asset

One important use is to establish the current position of an operating asset before assessing a new project. This means understanding what the operation consumes, produces and emits, what obligations it faces, and who controls the relevant equipment and contracts.

Energy

What it burns and what it buys

Fuel mix and quantity, self-generation versus purchased electricity, reconstructed average load, and where the asset sits relative to the network. We establish the fuel type from evidence rather than assuming it from the industry code, which is wrong often enough to matter.

Exposure

Estimated 2029 compliance exposure

We project the facility's Safeguard baseline and estimate its future compliance position against a revenue proxy. The calculation includes the statutory baseline floor, which standard projections run straight through, and the period in which additional abatement may not generate credits.

Control

Who can actually decide

Ownership and operator structure. The power station often belongs to a third party under a long-term contract, and its fuel is reported under a different industry entirely. Contract end dates where they are disclosed.

Position

Where it sits in its sector

We compare the asset with similar operations, then check whether the facility-level results add up to the published sector total. Differences between the two can reveal classification, reporting or scope errors.

The energy and emissions inventory

The inventory is built once and used by the asset, project, investment and sector work alike:

  • Fuel and purchased-electricity inventory
  • Self-generation and average-load reconstruction
  • Scope 1 and, where supportable, relevant Scope 2 context
  • Safeguard baseline and decline-path exposure
  • Facility-to-sector reconciliation
  • Peer benchmarking, with uncertainty and evidence grades attached

Method

Built from facility records and checked against independent data

We first reconstruct the asset from regulatory filings, licences and company disclosures. We then compare the result with an independently assembled account, such as national energy statistics, a pollutant inventory or trade data. Material differences are investigated and reported.

Two figures only confirm each other if they come from separate sources. A facility’s reported emissions and the regulator’s published intensity factor will always agree, because one is calculated from the other. Comparing them proves nothing, and it is an easy mistake to make. Every cross-check we report names both sets of inputs and shows that they do not overlap.

Evidence labels

How we label the evidence

Regulatory filing Reported by the operator to a regulator. The strongest source available. Still checked for unit and scope errors, which we find regularly.
Licence record Named in a state licence or permit. Establishes who operates a plant and often what equipment is installed.
Company disclosure Company reporting, investor material, sustainability data. Useful and specific, but not independent of the operator.
Calculated Calculated from reported quantities. Labelled as calculated wherever it is used.
Owner data required Held by the operator and not published. It goes on the owner-data request list rather than being estimated.
Not publicly available We have established that the information is not published by any party. It is recorded as unavailable, not treated as zero.

What public sources will not give you

Some information is not available from public sources and can only be confirmed by the owner or another project party. Any estimate made without it should be clearly identified as an assumption.

Not availableWhy it matters
Interval load curveNothing can be sized or priced without it
Peak demandDetermines connection and equipment, not average load
Unit run hours and standby strategySeparates a continuous plant from a backup set
Contract price and volume termsDecides who captures a saving, which often decides whether anything happens at all
