Submission · Senate inquiry · August 2026

Australia regulates data centres through multiple systems, but cannot trace one facility across them

Energy reporting, grid connection, planning, environmental, water and rating systems each record part of the same facility. They use different identifiers and reporting boundaries, and no public crosswalk shows which records refer to the same physical site or which rules apply to it.

The regulatory gap examined

The submission asks whether Australia’s existing regulatory systems can identify data-centre facilities consistently, show which rules apply to them, and produce comparable information about their energy and water use. It addresses term of reference (a) and the energy and water elements of term of reference (b); other community, industry and biodiversity impacts are outside its scope.

Australia regulates data centres through several systems. A public facility-level crosswalk is needed to connect their records, regulatory coverage and measurement boundaries.

Key findings

  1. No public crosswalk was found connecting the records for the same data-centre facility.

    NGER reporting, grid connection, planning, environmental approval, water and rating systems use different names, identifiers and facility boundaries. In the public sources reviewed, a reader cannot reliably determine which records refer to the same physical site.

  2. Most data-centre records in the NABERS extract used a scope that excludes IT load.

    Ten of the 11 data-centre records returned by the 15 August extract used the Infrastructure rating scope. A subsequent validity check found that six of the 11 ratings remained current, five of them on the same scope. Because the Infrastructure scope excludes IT equipment, the rating cannot by itself show the total energy performance of the data centre. That a public register can lose five of 11 entries in a month without any facility closing is itself the case for a defined reporting boundary.

  3. The proposed 30 MW threshold sits in the National Electricity Rules, which do not operate in Western Australia or the Northern Territory.

    The AEMC’s Package 2 draft places the threshold in rules that apply only in NEM jurisdictions. The policy advice reviewed did not identify an equivalent pathway for non-NEM, off-grid or islanded facilities.

  4. Published water figures do not use a consistent measurement boundary.

    One company’s FY2025 reporting labels the same 773 ML figure as both “consumption” and “withdrawal”. Without a common definition, figures from different facilities or reporting systems cannot be compared reliably.

  5. The EPBC industry classification did not describe the principal energy infrastructure in one proposal reviewed.

    A proposed data-centre development including approximately 1,038 MW of gas-fired generation was recorded under a renewable-energy industry category. Industry-category searches are therefore an unreliable way to identify all data-centre proposals.

    Updated 14 September 2026 from the referral document; the lodged submission used an earlier industry-media range of 900–1,100 MW. The proposal was determined a controlled action on 4 September 2026.

Recommendations

  1. Connect the facility records

    Assign a persistent facility identifier and publish a crosswalk connecting the non-sensitive NGER, connection, planning, environmental, water and rating records.

  2. Connect the facility to its regulatory perimeter

    Publish which energy, environmental and water frameworks apply to each facility, including NEM, non-NEM and off-grid sites.

  3. Connect the reported measures to a common boundary

    Define whether each energy or water figure covers infrastructure, IT equipment, withdrawal or consumption before facilities are compared.

Open questions

Seven matters are listed in the submission as unverified, including the correspondence between NABERS star ratings and PUE values, any published timetable for NABERS work on a data centre water framework, the final connection arrangement for one of the two Northern Territory proposals, and the water and planning instruments of all eight jurisdictions. The Safeguard Mechanism's Schedule 1 contains no production variable for a data centre; the consequence for an off-grid facility is set out as an uncertainty. No legal advice was obtained.
