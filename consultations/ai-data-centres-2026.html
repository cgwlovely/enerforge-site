Submission · Senate inquiry · August 2026

Australia has the data on data centres, but cannot reliably link it by facility

Australia already collects data-centre information through energy, environmental and rating systems. The records use different facility boundaries and identifiers, so it is difficult to tell which rules apply to the same physical site.

Data update · 14 September 2026 The 15 August extract returned 11 data-centre records. A subsequent check of rating validity found that six remained current and five were no longer listed. Rating expiry explains at least one of the five (certificate expiry 25 August 2026) and is the likely cause of the rest; a difference in filtering between the two extracts has not been excluded. The lodged submission reports the original extract result; this page shows both the original and the subsequently verified counts.

The question

Term of reference (a): the effectiveness of existing regulatory frameworks in managing the growth of data centres; and the energy and water elements of term of reference (b). Community, industry and biodiversity impacts, and the arrangements between government and AI companies, are outside the submission.

Key findings

  1. NGER, connection, planning, environmental and water records each identify a data centre differently.

    Substantial regulatory data exists; it cannot be read reliably for one site.

  2. Ten of the 11 data-centre records returned by the extract used the Infrastructure rating scope.

    The scope excludes IT load, so a rating on it says little about a data centre’s energy performance. A subsequent validity check found that six of the 11 ratings remained current; five of those six are also on the Infrastructure scope. That a public register can lose five of 11 entries in a month without any facility closing is itself the case for a defined reporting boundary.

  3. The proposed 30 MW National Electricity Rules threshold would not cover facilities in Western Australia or the Northern Territory.

    The AEMC’s Package 2 draft places it in rules that apply in the NEM jurisdictions only; the 51-page policy advice contains no reference to off-grid, islanded or unconnected facilities.

  4. Published water metrics are not comparable.

    One company's FY25 reports label the same 773 ML both “consumed” and “withdrawal”.

  5. Federal classification fields are unreliable.

    A proposed facility including approximately 1,038 MW of gas-fired generation is recorded on the EPBC portal under a renewable industry type.

    Updated 14 September 2026 from the referral document; the lodged submission used an earlier industry-media range of 900–1,100 MW. The proposal was determined a controlled action on 4 September 2026.

Recommendations

  1. Identify the facility

    Create a persistent identifier across NGER, connection, planning, environmental and water records, with a public crosswalk of the non-sensitive fields.

  2. Publish the regulatory perimeter

    Show which framework applies to NEM-connected, non-NEM and off-grid facilities, so that a site’s obligations can be read from one map.

  3. Define the measurement boundary

    Specify whether energy and water measures cover infrastructure or IT load, and withdrawal or consumption, before any volume is reported or compared.

Open questions

Seven matters are listed in the submission as unverified, including the correspondence between NABERS star ratings and PUE values, any published timetable for NABERS work on a data centre water framework, the final connection arrangement for one of the two Northern Territory proposals, and the water and planning instruments of all eight jurisdictions. The Safeguard Mechanism's Schedule 1 contains no production variable for a data centre; the consequence for an off-grid facility is set out as an uncertainty. No legal advice was obtained.

Sources

Evidence used

NGER Act 2007, NGER Regulations 2008 and the NGER (Safeguard Mechanism) Rule 2015 as currently compiled (legislation). AEMC advice on data centre regulatory pathways and the Package 2 draft determination (regulator document). EPBC Act Public Portal referral 2026/10560 and supporting documents (regulatory filing). NABERS Rating Register, complete ratings extract of 4,658 rows downloaded on 15 August 2026, filtered to the data-centre building type (public register); validity re-checked against a fresh extract on 14 September 2026. Directive (EU) 2023/1791 Article 12 (legislation). Company annual and sustainability reporting (company disclosure).
