Submission · Senate inquiry · August 2026

Australia has the data on data centres, but cannot reliably link it by facility

Australia already collects data-centre information through energy, environmental and rating systems. The records use different facility boundaries and identifiers, so it is difficult to tell which rules apply to the same physical site.

InstitutionSenate Environment and Communications References Committee
InquiryArtificial intelligence and data centres, referred 13 May 2026
StatusLodged August 2026 · published by the committee as Submission 89 · report due 16 November 2026
ScopeTerm of reference (a), and the energy and water elements of (b)
11Australian data centres hold a current NABERS Energy rating: eight in NSW, two in Victoria, one in WA
4,658rows in the complete NABERS current-ratings extract reviewed, 15 August 2026
3recommendations, all concerned with how records are structured rather than how strict any standard is

The question

Term of reference (a): the effectiveness of existing regulatory frameworks in managing the growth of data centres; and the energy and water elements of term of reference (b). Community, industry and biodiversity impacts, and the arrangements between government and AI companies, are outside the submission.

What the evidence shows

  1. NGER, connection, planning, environmental and water records each identify a data centre differently.

    Substantial regulatory data exists; it cannot be read reliably for one site.

  2. Ten of the eleven rated facilities are rated on the Infrastructure scope, which excludes IT load.

    A rating that excludes the load that defines a data centre says little about its energy performance.

  3. The 30 MW threshold in the AEMC's Package 2 draft does not reach the Northern Territory or Western Australia.

    It sits in the National Electricity Rules, which apply to the NEM jurisdictions. The 51-page policy advice contains no reference to off-grid, islanded or unconnected facilities.

  4. Published water metrics are not comparable.

    One company's FY25 reports label the same 773 ML both “consumed” and “withdrawal”.

  5. Federal classification fields are unreliable.

    A proposed facility including approximately 1,038 MW of gas-fired generation is recorded on the EPBC portal under a renewable industry type.

Recommendations

Identify the facilityA persistent identifier across NGER, connection, planning, environmental and water records, with a public crosswalk of non-sensitive fields.
Show who regulates itA published coverage map for NEM-connected, non-NEM and off-grid facilities.
Measure consistentlyDefine the reporting boundary before the volume.

What remains unresolved

Seven matters are listed in the submission as unverified, including the correspondence between NABERS star ratings and PUE values, any published timetable for NABERS work on a data centre water framework, the final connection arrangement for one of the two Northern Territory proposals, and the water and planning instruments of all eight jurisdictions. The Safeguard Mechanism's Schedule 1 contains no production variable for a data centre; the consequence for an off-grid facility is set out as an uncertainty. No legal advice was obtained.

Correction, 14 September 2026: the referral states the gas-fired capacity as approximately 1,038 MW; an earlier version quoted an industry-media range of 900–1,100 MW. The proposal was determined a controlled action on 4 September 2026. The count of rated facilities is as at the date of the extract; a later check found six, the difference being expired ratings.

Sources

Evidence used

AEMC advice on data centre regulatory pathways and the Package 2 draft determination (regulator document). EPBC Act Public Portal referral 2026/10560 and supporting documents (regulatory filing). Company annual and sustainability reporting (company disclosure).
