Submission · DCCEEW · September 2026

Set the post-2030 decline rate and the trade-exposed pathway together

The Department can reproduce the starting point from public facility data. Using the published register, we calculate a starting decline rate of 3.53% a year for the 62% target and 5.99% for the 70% target under current rules. If the rate for trade-exposed facilities falls by one percentage point, the other 187 facilities must reduce by about 0.2 percentage points more. Many rate combinations meet the same target; policy determines how the burden is shared.

The question addressed

The consultation paper states that the decline rate for 2030-31 to 2034-35 must be calibrated to meet the agreed target, and lists the factors that calibration has to take into account: the target, production at existing facilities, facility entry and exit, uptake of concessional baselines, policy changes from the review, the reserve and credits issued to below-threshold facilities. The published estimates (RepuTex, EY and Australian Energy Producers) give different ranges for the final rate, and their published methods generally do not separate the mechanical effect of the Rule from assumptions about production, abatement, entry and exit. This submission separates the result produced by the Rule from later assumptions about production, abatement, new facilities and closures. It gives a rule-based starting point from public data, the adjustments that connect it to a final rate, and a way to test those adjustments after implementation.

Key findings

  1. The default decline rate and the post-2030 trade-exposed pathway must be calibrated together.

    For a given 2035 target, each combination of the two rates that meets the target lies on one iso-target curve. Reducing the trade-exposed rate by one percentage point a year increases the default rate required of the other 187 facilities by approximately 0.2 points a year.

  2. Under current rules the rule-based starting point is 3.53% a year for the 62% target and 5.99% for the 70% target.

    The starting point is calculated from the FY2024-25 register, the Rule’s own formulas, published commodity projections and a limited set of stated scenario conventions, and can be reproduced from the inputs listed in the submission. At the legislated indicative rate of 3.285% the starting point reaches FY2034-35 net emissions of 70.25 Mt, 1.4 Mt above the 62% point target of 68.8 Mt.

  3. The difference from the Department’s projection of 66 Mt comes from stated assumptions rather than from the Rule.

    The Department’s series is a behavioural projection that includes expected onsite abatement; the starting point assumes none. Annex B reconciles the two series year by year and attributes the gap to production, entry, exit and abatement assumptions.

  4. Publicly visible new entrants would add about 17.0 Mt a year of covered emissions against 2.59 Mt of baseline once all operate.

    The pipeline is 41 coal mines, 5 LNG trains and 16 iron ore mines in public trackers, entered on a phased profile. The calibration should list large known projects separately, including the off-grid data-centre proposal examined in Annex F, instead of including them in a general growth assumption.

  5. The annual register can test the main assumptions.

    The Department should publish how emissions intensity changed across existing facilities, by industry (Annex G). That measure shows whether onsite abatement is tracking the path assumed in the calibration. The Department should also state in advance when it would recalibrate the rate.

  6. The covered population is 208 facilities.

    The published register has 228 rows. Removing the 19 rows the Regulator marks as eligible facilities under Rule s 58B leaves 209 rows, and Telfer Gold Mine occupies two of them after a mid-year change of operator. Covered emissions are 132.8 Mt on either count. The Safeguard Atlas recomputes the submission’s published figures in the browser from the same inputs.

Recommendation

Adopt and publish a framework for post-2030 calibration that others can reproduce. Use three published steps and review them regularly.

  1. Establish the starting point

    Set the default rate and the trade-exposed rate together, either recomputed by the Department on the same assumptions or taken from the rule-based starting point in this submission. State whether the compliance condition is the FY2034–35 point target or the five-year budget.

  2. Quantify the adjustments

    Before the period begins, publish the effect of each adjustment separately: production, entry and exit, trade-exposed treatment, onsite abatement, below-threshold credits and the reserve. State how much each one moves the rate, and list large publicly identified projects individually.

  3. Monitor outcomes and recalibrate

    Publish the relative-intensity effect of covered facilities with each annual register, compare it with the abatement pathway used in the calibration, and set out the recalibration mechanism in advance.

Open questions

Annex E lists the inputs the public record cannot establish, and who holds them. None is estimated; each is handled by a stated convention.

  • Post-2030 production at existing facilities.
  • The commissioning probability and timing of new projects.
  • Closures of non-coal facilities beyond the four confirmed.
  • Trade-exposed applications and renewals after 2030.
  • Policy changes arising from the review.
  • Credits issued to below-threshold facilities.
  • The size and composition of the reserve.

Annex H.2 records the limits of the evidence base.

  • The closure search cannot show that the list is exhaustive.
  • The Regulator publishes neither stable facility identifiers nor facility-level production, so 61 candidate identity matches await manual review.
  • Assuming full compliance puts the modelled base year about 1.7 Mt below the reported outcome.
  • Phasing in the project pipeline puts the modelled series 1–3 Mt below the Department’s projection from FY2026–27 to FY2028–29.
  • The minimum baseline is applied only to facilities with a base-year baseline of at least 50,000 t.
