Methods

How the analysis is built and checked

An asset is reconstructed from its own records, tested against data assembled a different way, and reported with each figure labelled as reported, calculated or assumed.

Step 1

1. Reconstruct the asset

The asset is reconstructed from regulatory filings, licences and company disclosures: what it consumes, burns, emits, produces and pays, and who owns and operates each part.

Step 2

2. Test it against independent data

The result is compared with an independently assembled account, such as national energy statistics, a pollutant inventory or trade data. Material differences are investigated and reported.

Two figures only confirm each other if they come from separate sources. A facility’s reported emissions and the regulator’s published intensity factor will always agree, because one is calculated from the other. Comparing them proves nothing, and it is an easy mistake to make. Every cross-check we report names both sets of inputs and shows that they do not overlap.

Step 3

3. Separate reported, calculated and assumed values

Regulatory filing Reported by the operator to a regulator. Usually the strongest public source, but still checked for unit and scope differences.
Licence record Named in a state licence or permit. Establishes who operates a plant and often what equipment is installed.
Company disclosure Company reporting, investor material, sustainability data. Useful and specific, but not independent of the operator.
Calculated Calculated from reported quantities. Labelled as calculated wherever it is used.
Owner data required Held by the operator and not published. It goes on the owner-data request list.
Not found in public sources We could not find the information in the public sources reviewed. It is recorded as not found, not treated as zero.

4. Record uncertainty and missing information

Some information is not available from public sources and can only be confirmed by the owner or another project party. Any estimate made without it should be clearly identified as an assumption.

Not availableWhy it matters
Interval load curveDetailed system sizing and pricing require it
Peak demandDetermines connection and equipment, not average load
Unit run hours and standby strategySeparates a continuous plant from a backup set
Contract price and volume termsShows how savings are shared and whether the commercial structure is workable

Work can end in four results: continue, redesign, supplement the data, or stop.

The records

One linked account per asset

Questions about policy, investment, assets and energy often depend on the same underlying information. A facility belongs to a company, uses energy, reports emissions, operates under approvals and may host one or more projects. We link those records so that each analysis starts from a consistent account of the asset.

Company ↔Facility ↔Project ↔Energy ↔Emissions ↔Grid ↔Technology ↔Approval ↔Capital

The value comes from showing how the records relate to one another, not from collecting them in isolation. Each relationship is supported by a source and given an evidence label.

Consultation submissions

How submissions are prepared

A submission is made where the public record can answer a specific question a consultation asks: which facilities or sectors a proposed rule change reaches and by how much; whether a stated national or sectoral quantity reconciles bottom-up; where a mechanism would and would not reach in practice; what the record can establish about an industrial population. Legal interpretation, positions advocating a commercial outcome, and questions that need confidential operator data are out of scope.

Every published submission records the question examined, the evidence used, the findings, the recommendations and what remains unresolved, followed by sources, the PDF and related research. Where a published figure cannot be reproduced, the difference is stated. Submissions are independent contributions on Heliovulcan's own behalf; the bodies whose processes are monitored (CCA, DCCEEW, AEMO, AER, AEMC, parliamentary committees) neither appoint nor endorse it.
