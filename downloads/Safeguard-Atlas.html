What the model shows

Covered emissions and baselines diverge sharply after 2030

Baselines fall every year by rule. Covered emissions do not. Under the starting-point assumptions the gap between the two widens from FY2029-30, and the units that gap requires facilities to surrender are the scheme's real obligation.

The chart separates reported covered emissions, aggregate baselines and modelled compliant net emissions, the smaller of each facility's covered emissions and its baseline summed across facilities. The difference between covered and compliant emissions is the number of units facilities would need to surrender under the settings in force. The dashed line is current policy on the starting-point assumptions.

What has changed

The rule alone explains most of last year's fall in baselines

Two registers have now been published under the reformed rules. For each facility continuing under the same name, moving its FY2023-24 baseline by the change in its ERC and nothing else lands close to the published FY2024-25 figure. What remains is production and intensity. The same comparison will be run against each register as it is published.

What changes the result

There is no single correct post-2030 decline rate

The rate required to meet a 2035 target changes materially with assumptions about industrial production, new projects, closures and the treatment of the 21 facilities with a trade-exposed determination. Hold the target fixed and those settings trade against each other: relief for one group must be made up by another. Set them below; the rate required, the curve and the decomposition follow.

Limitations

  • The model is based on the FY2024-25 register. A facility that has not yet reported is not represented except through the new-entrant setting.
  • Facility baselines are the published values scaled by the change in the ERC. They are not re-derived from each facility's production and intensity, so a facility whose production changes materially will diverge from the modelled path until the next register is published.
  • Production assumptions are national commodity paths applied to every facility in that commodity. An individual facility may move in the opposite direction.
  • The paths for facilities with a trade-exposed determination are those the Regulator has published. The determinations expire, and their treatment after expiry is a policy setting rather than an input.
  • The results are calculations on stated assumptions, not a compliance determination or a forecast.
